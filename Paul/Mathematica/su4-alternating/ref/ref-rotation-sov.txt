# gl(3), L=2, adjoint⊗adjoint: a global rotation of the SoV vacuum as a product of Q-determinants

The L=1 results are in `Rotation-e8-SoV.wb` (Sections 10–10e):
- **Decomposition.** Every generic rotation of $x_0$ is $\Omega_w\,\rho((y_1-G(z)^T)^{-1})$.
- **The resolvent factor.** The resolvent acts on $\Psi_B$ by $\prod_i(1-z_i/y_1)^{-\mu_i}$. This factor equals a $2\times2$ determinant in $(Q^3,Q^2)$ with the insertion $y_1^{-x/h}$, divided by the $2\times2$ minor of $\Psi_Q(x_0)$.

**Here (L=2, diagonal frame).**
- $x_0=(e_1^TP_{\rm phys}^{-1})^{\otimes2}$ is a product state. So the same rotation on each site is $\Phi_2=(\Omega_{zw}\,\rho(D))^{\otimes2}$, with $D={\rm diag}(1/(y_1-z_i))$, and $\Phi_2\cdot\Psi_B=\prod_i(y_1-z_i)^{-\mu_i}\,\Omega\cdot\Psi_B$.
- The second factor is the $6\times6$ formula of `L2-diagonal-frame-overlap.wb`.
- **Question.** Is the first factor an su(2)-like determinant?

The setup cells are copied verbatim from `L2-diagonal-frame-overlap.wb` (E basis). The numeric point is $h=1/2$, $\theta=(2/7,-3/5)$, $z=(3/2,5/4,2/3)$, $w=(5/2,7/3,3/5)$.

JJ[k_, l_, rep_] := JJ[k, l, rep] = Which[

opDir[tt_, Q_, x_] := nuP[1][x] nuP[1][x + hN] nuP[1][x + 2 hN] Q[x - hN] - (tt[[1]] /. u -> x) nuP[1][x + hN] nuP[1][x + 2 hN] Q[x] + (tt[[2]] /. u -> x + hN) nuP[1][x + 2 hN] Q[x + hN] - (tt[[3]] /. u -> x + 2 hN) Q[x + 2 hN];

## 1. The rotated SoV vacuum at L=2
- **The rotation.** At L=1 in the diagonal frame, a generic rotation of $x_0^{(1)}$ is $\Omega_{zw}\,\rho(D)$, with $D={\rm diag}(1/(y_1-z_i))$. This is the companion-frame resolvent conjugated by $P_{\rm phys}$. The same rotation on both sites is $\Phi_2=(\Omega_{zw}\rho(D))^{\otimes2}=\Omega\,\rho(D)^{\otimes2}$.
- **Overlap.** $\rho(D)^{\otimes2}$ is diagonal on each weight sector, so $\Phi_2\cdot\Psi_B=\prod_i(y_1-z_i)^{-\mu_i(B)}\,\Omega\cdot\Psi_B$.
- **Candidate su(2)-like factor, the L=2 version of Rotation-e8-SoV 10e.**
$$\frac{\det_{4\times4}\big[-\langle Q^jE\,x^{\beta-1}\rangle_k,\ \langle Q^{j[-2]}E\,x^{\beta-1}\rangle_k\big]_{(j,k)}}{\prod_s\big[Q^2(\theta_s-2h)Q^3(\theta_s-3h)-Q^3(\theta_s-2h)Q^2(\theta_s-3h)\big]}\ \stackrel{?}{\propto}\ \prod_i\Big(1-\frac{z_i}{y_1}\Big)^{-\mu_i}.$$
  Here $E=y_1^{-x/h}$, rows are $j\in\{3,2\}\times k\in\{1,2\}$, and columns are $\{-\langle PE\rangle,\langle P^{[-2]}E\rangle\}\times\{1,x\}$.
- **Cross-check.** The degenerate $6\times6$, with $F_1=E$ and $F_2=E$ on $\{\theta_s-2h,\theta_s-h\}$ and 0 on $\{\theta_s,\theta_s+h\}$.

## 2. Result
For the same global rotation on each site, $\Phi_2=x_0\,\rho(g)^{\otimes2}$ with $g\leftrightarrow(w,y_1)$:
$$\frac{\langle\Phi_2|\Psi_B\rangle}{\langle x_0|\Psi_B\rangle}\ \propto\ \underbrace{\frac{\det_{6\times6}N[Q_B,Q_{{\rm vac},w}]}{\Psi_Q^B(x_0)}}_{\langle\Omega_{zw}|\Psi\rangle\text{ formula}}\ \times\ \underbrace{\frac{\det_{4\times4}\big[-\langle Q^jE\,x^{\beta-1}\rangle_k,\ \langle Q^{j[-2]}E\,x^{\beta-1}\rangle_k\big]_{j\in\{3,2\}}}{\prod_s\big[Q^2(\theta_s-2h)Q^3(\theta_s-3h)-Q^3(\theta_s-2h)Q^2(\theta_s-3h)\big]}}_{=\prod_i(1-z_i/y_1)^{-\mu_i},\ E=y_1^{-x/h}}$$
- **Precision.** Each identity holds for all 64 states to about $10^{-50}$, for $y_1=-6/5$, $3$ and $5/7+i/3$.
- **The su(2)-like factor is $4\times4$, i.e. $2L\times2L$.** Its rows are the two dual Q's $Q^3,Q^2$ times the two brackets $k$, and its columns are $\{-\langle PE\rangle,\langle P^{[-2]}E\rangle\}\times\{1,x\}$.
  - At L=1 it is $2\times2$.
  - It is the y₂→∞ degeneration of the $6\times6$ vacuum determinant: $F_2\to E$ on $\{\theta_s-2h,\theta_s-h\}$, checked directly. The column operation that removes the $F_1$ rows from the $\langle PF^{[2]}\rangle$ columns reduces it to $4\times4$.

## 4. Result: Q-function representation of the site-1 rotation
In the companion frame, write $x_0=e_1^T\otimes e_1^T$ and $\Phi=(e_1^T\rho(g))\otimes e_1^T$.

**L=2 SoV basis.** The untwisted $B(u)$ has a simple spectrum on the 64 states. They form the full $8\times8$ grid (site-1 root triple, site-2 root triple), each ladder carrying the 8 L=1 patterns.

**L=2 SoV wavefunction**, exact for all 64 SoV states and all 64 eigenstates, $\sim10^{-48}$, and for several row/$k$ choices:
$$\frac{\langle x|\Psi_B\rangle}{\langle x_0|\Psi_B\rangle}=N_x\prod_{s=1,2}\frac{\det_2[v(x_{s,21}),v(x_{s,1})]\,v_k(x_{s,22})}{(\text{same at the vacuum roots }\theta_s-3h,\theta_s-2h,\theta_s-h)},\qquad v=(Q^1,Q^2,Q^3)\text{ of the L=2 state}.$$

**Expansion of $\Phi$.** Only 12 SoV states appear:
- the 8 states with site 2 at the vacuum roots;
- 4 states with site 2 at $\{2|20\}$, where $x_{2,22}$ moves from $\theta_2-h$ to $\theta_2$. Their site-2 factor is $\lambda_2(B)=v_k(\theta_2)/v_k(\theta_2-h)$, which does not depend on $k$ because $v(\theta_2)\parallel v(\theta_2-h)$.

The result is
$$\frac{\langle\Phi|\Psi_B\rangle}{\langle x_0|\Psi_B\rangle}=\sum_{\nu}c^{L=1}_\nu(g)\,W_\nu(B),\qquad W_\nu(B)=\sum_{x}K_{\nu x}\,\psi^{(1)}_{x_1}(B)\,\lambda_2(B)^{[x_2\neq{\rm vac}]},$$
where:
- $c^{L=1}_\nu(g)$ are the L=1 SoV coefficients of $e_1^T\rho(g)$, i.e. the bilinear $c(a,b)$ of Rotation-e8-SoV Section 3;
- $\psi^{(1)}$ is the site-1 factor above, built from the L=2 Q's;
- $K$ is $g$-independent, sparse and rational. In the normalization $K_{\{2|21\},\rm vac}=1$:

| $\nu$ | (site-1 pattern; site-2 vac) | (·; site-2 $\{2\|20\}$) |
|---|---|---|

| {1\|11} | {1\|11}: −4/9 | — |
| {2\|20} | {2\|20}: −1/2 | — |

**Reading the table.**
- At $h=1/2$ and $\theta_{12}=31/35$, the 31s and 35s are $t=\theta_{12}/h=62/35$. The site-2 coupling is therefore rational in $t$.
- The four $\nu$ with a single entry are exactly those for which $x_\nu\otimes e_1^T$ is already a B-eigencovector.

**Open.**
- The closed form of $K$ in $t$, which needs a second numeric point.
- Whether $\sum_\nu c_\nu(w,y_1)W_\nu$ resums to a compact determinant, e.g. ladder-1 brackets of the L=2 Q's times the su(2)-like factor, as it does at L=1.

**Bracket product form in the $(\alpha,\beta,\lambda_1)$ variables.** A ladder-1 bracket is $\langle Q^jF\rangle=\sum_mF(m)v_j(\theta_1+mh)=v_j(-3)[F(-3)+\alpha m_F]+v_j(-2)[F(-2)+\beta m_F]$ with $m_F=F(-1)+\lambda_1F(0)$. So a Cauchy–Binet-type object (bra wavefunction $\det_2[F_1,F_2]\,G$) has the product form
$$P=\big[a_0+a_1\alpha+a_2\beta+\lambda_1(b_1\alpha+b_2\beta)\big]\,(g_0+g_1\lambda_1).$$
For a group polynomial $P=p_0+p_\alpha\alpha+p_\beta\beta+\lambda_1(q_0+q_\alpha\alpha+q_\beta\beta)+\lambda_1^2(r_\alpha\alpha+r_\beta\beta)$ this is equivalent to the two conditions
$$q_\alpha p_0q_0-q_0^2p_\alpha-p_0^2r_\alpha=0,\qquad q_\beta p_0q_0-q_0^2p_\beta-p_0^2r_\beta=0 .$$

### 8 — Conclusions (2026-10-09)
1. **Compact exact form.** For any site-1 covector $\sum_\nu c_\nu x_\nu\otimes e_1^T$, the overlap over $\langle x_0|\Psi_B\rangle$ is the polynomial in $(\alpha,\beta,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ of cell 61 (exact, $10^{-47}$, all 64 states). It is $\sum_x\varphi^{(1)}_x\psi_x+\lambda_2\sum_x\varphi^{(2)}_x\psi_x$ with $\varphi^{(i)}=c\cdot K^{(i)}$ ($K^{\rm vac}$ rank 8, $K^{20}$ rank 4).
2. **Neither group is a rotation**: $X(c\cdot K^{(i)})$ has three nonzero singular values for generic rotations $c$. So no site-2 group is an L=1-type determinant with transformed bra data.
3. **The L=1 $\det_3\times\det_2$ is not a polynomial identity.** On ladder 1 of the L=2 states it is not even in the span of the 8 site-1 wavefunctions (0.11). At L=1 every function of the 8 states is in that span, which is why it works there.
4. **Locality classifies the usable determinants.** For the L=1-type $3\times3$, a ladder-1 bracket determinant is a site-1 object iff $F_2\propto F_1$ on $\{\theta_1-2h,\theta_1-h\}$ (exact). The resulting family has a **4-dimensional image** (explicit cubic map, ideal of 10 quadrics); three independent bra functions do not enlarge it (max rank 4 over 32 components). The groups of generic rotations are not in it (exact Gröbner membership: 0.16–3.9 vs 0 for members; numeric fits 0.18, 0.014 vs $10^{-17}$ for a member).
5. **Sums of two local $3\times3$ determinants cover the whole 8-dim local space** (random vectors fit to $10^{-15}$), so a 'sum of four determinants' form exists for every site-1 covector and is uninformative.

**Verdict.** Within ladder-1 bracket determinants of the natural type, the site-1 rotation has no single-determinant form; the exact representation is the 12-state sum in the compact form of item 1. A single-determinant form, if it exists, must use a different structure: brackets involving $v(\theta_1+h)$ (outside the 2-plane), both ladders with insertions producing $\lambda_2$, or larger determinants with locality by cancellation.

### 9 — Conclusions (2026-10-09)
1. **No contour-integral determinant with ladder-dependent vacuum data.** For $\Omega_w\otimes\Omega_{w'}$ the full-measure $\det_6$ with $F$ = $w$-vacuum Q's on ladder 1 and $w'$-vacuum Q's on ladder 2 fails (max relative deviation 24.9 with the L=2 vacuum Q's, 5.8 with the one-site ones, 6.1 mixed), while the sanity check $\Omega_{w'}\otimes\Omega_{w'}\propto\det_6[F_{{\rm vac},w'}]$ holds to $10^{-48}$. ## 11. Beyond the $(w_1,y)$ family: the ket-twist torus (2026-10-09, later)

**Question (Paul).** Can the construction of §10 be generalized to states beyond the vacuum, i.e. more general rotations on each of the two sites?

**Tool.** The Cartan torus of the ket twist, $\rho(D)\otimes\rho(D)$ with $D=\mathrm{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3)$, commutes with every twisted transfer matrix and acts on an eigenstate $\Psi_B$ of total weight $\mu$ by the character $\chi_\mu(D)=\prod_i d_i^{\mu_i}$ (for a resolvent $D=(y_1-G^T)^{-1}$ this character is the su(2)-like $\det_4$ of §2). So every product state with a determinant overlap comes with its whole torus orbit for free: $\langle v_1\rho(D)\otimes v_2\rho(D)|\Psi_B\rangle=\chi_\mu(D)\,\langle v_1\otimes v_2|\Psi_B\rangle$ (cell 108).

**Expectation to test.** The §10 family is $v_2=\Omega_{(w_1,y)}$, $v_1=v_2\,\rho(D_{w_2(y)})$ with $w_2(y)=y\,(\theta_{12}-2h)/(\theta_{12}-h)$; its torus orbit is $\{(g\,\rho(D_{w_2(y)}),\,g)\}$ with $g=\Omega_{(w_1,y)}\rho(D)$. If $(w_1,D)\mapsto\Omega_{(w_1,y)}\rho(D)$ at fixed $y$ were dominant onto the 3-dimensional rotation orbit, every rotation $g$ would be $\Omega_{(w_1,y)}\rho(D)$ with $y$ free, and $(g\rho(D_\kappa),g)$ would have a determinant formula for every $\kappa$ — the paper's $\kappa$-family. **It is not** (cell 108): at fixed $y$ the Jacobian has rank 3 = torus + scale, i.e. $w_1$ is itself a torus direction ($\Omega_{(w_1,y)}/\Omega_{(w_1',y)}$ is a torus character, exactly) and $y$ is a torus **invariant** of the rotation. Hence every rotation $g$ on site 2 is $\Omega_{(w_1,y(g))}\rho(D)$ with $y(g)$ unique, and its site-1 partner $g\,\rho((w_2(y(g))-G^T)^{-1})$ is unique:
$$\langle g\,\rho((w_2(y(g))-G^T)^{-1})\otimes g\,|\Psi_B\rangle \ \propto\ \chi_\mu(D)\ \frac{\det_6N[Q_B,Q_{\rm vac,(w_1,w_2(y(g)))}]}{\Psi_Q}\ \frac{\det_2^{(1)}[Q_B;y(g)]}{\mathrm{minor}_1}.$$
